## Why this exists

If you run a food business, you already know the drill: fridge, freezer, and hot-hold
temperatures need to be written down every day, in a form you can hand an inspector without
digging through old notebooks. Most places still do this on a paper sheet taped to the
fridge door. It works right up until the sheet gets greasy, the pen disappears, or the
inspector asks for last March.

This app does one job. It replaces the paper sheet with something faster, and it turns your
history into a clean PDF on demand. It is not a full HACCP management suite with checklists
for everything, and it doesn't try to be. If you want the fastest way to log temperatures
and stay inspection-ready, this is it.

## Why not just keep the paper log?

Paper is fine until it isn't. Some honest reasons kitchens switch:

- **Retrieval.** When an inspector asks for records from three months ago, a PDF export
  beats flipping through a binder of curled sheets.
- **Legibility.** A number scrawled at 6am during a delivery rush is not always a number
  anyone can read later.
- **Catching problems.** Paper doesn't tell you that your dessert fridge has been creeping
  up half a degree a week. An app that flags out-of-range readings does.
- **Continuity.** Sheets run out, get lost, or get soup on them. A log on your phone is just
  there.

What paper still has going for it: it never runs out of battery and anyone can use it.
That's why the app copies the paper workflow instead of reinventing it. If your staff can
fill in a sheet, they can use this.

## Temperature basics: fridge, freezer, and hot-hold

If you're wondering what temperatures you should actually be logging, here's the short
version. Always confirm against your local regulator's guidance, since exact requirements
vary by country and sometimes by state or local authority.

| Unit | Common guideline | What you're avoiding |
|---|---|---|
| Fridge | At or below 5°C (41°F) is the widely used target for chilled storage. | Bacteria multiplying in perishable food held too warm. |
| Freezer | Around -18°C (0°F) is the common standard for frozen storage. | Partial thawing and quality loss, plus growth if food warms enough. |
| Hot-hold | Food kept hot for service has a regulated minimum: 63°C in the UK, 135°F (57.2°C) in the US. Other regions differ. | Cooked food sitting in the temperature danger zone. |

The core idea behind all of it is the danger zone: the band between roughly fridge-cold and
hot-hold-hot where bacteria grow fastest. Cold storage keeps food below it, hot-holding
keeps food above it, and your log is the evidence that you checked. Most regulators expect
checks at least daily, and many businesses log morning and evening.

*This is general information, not food safety advice for your specific business. Your local
environmental health officer or health department has the numbers that apply to you.*

## What about HACCP?

Temperature records are one part of a HACCP-based food safety system, usually the part that
generates the most paperwork. This app covers that part: it's a HACCP-friendly temperature
log, not a complete HACCP plan builder. If you already have your plan (or use something like
Safer Food, Better Business in the UK), this slots in as the daily temperature diary. If you
need cleaning schedules, delivery checks, and full checklist management, you want a bigger
tool — this one is built to be the fastest at the thing you do every single day.

**Will an inspector accept a digital log?**

actually done and recorded consistently. If you're unsure, ask your inspector, but in
practice a clean digital log tends to go over better than a smudged sheet.
